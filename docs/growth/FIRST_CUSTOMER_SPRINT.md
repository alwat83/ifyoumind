## Ten-minute business demo and honest purchase invitation

1. Minute0–2: ask which cities, what decision date, what evidence is missing and what the buyer already uses. If only sites within one city are undecided, do not pitch a city report as a solution.
2. Minute2–5: show existing public sample, one source and its date/geography; explain city data and uncertainty. Invite the buyer to identify one useful finding and one gap.
3. Minute5–8: show the free-brief journey. Buyer controls registration and data entry. Do not promise live signup/email/payment has been independently verified end to end.
4. Minute8–10: if the report fits: “Would the fuller comparison be useful for your decision? It's $79 as a one-time report. Pro is $39/month and includes five reports if you prefer a subscription. You can also stop with the free brief.” No agent-initiated payment, discount, invented limited availability or custom-research service promise.

## Lightweight measurement and decision rules

Use the existing private buyer tracker; add anonymous IDs only as real interactions occur. Record approved asset, referral source, actual useful experience, decision need/timeframe, accepted demo, objection, optional checkout, independently funded settled purchase, feedback and permitted follow-up. No private bills in a tracker. A visitor is not a customer; an internal test or test payment is not traction. Do not add analytics to the isolated discovery entry or put household findings into a share URL.

Week-one learning targets: 10 meaningful outside-user experiences, five qualified buyer conversations, one independently paying customer. These are targets, not expected conversion rates or a promise. If five relevant demos produce no interest in purchase, inspect actual objections and free alternatives before building or discounting. If consumer sessions are interesting but unrelated to Business needs, keep the two journeys separate; do not claim a demonstrated paid funnel.
