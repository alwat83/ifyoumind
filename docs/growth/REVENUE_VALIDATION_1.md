## Single-purchase live protocol for founder approval

**Buyer B01 is pending designation and consent.** Select one willing independent adult with a real use for a city comparison. Keep names, consent record and reconciliation identifiers in restricted founder notes. An internal, reimbursed, or planned-refund purchase can validate operations. Exclude it from independent customer traction. No participant was contacted for this audit.

**Proposed question for approval:** “Should I open a specialty coffee shop in Denver, CO, or compare Austin, TX, Raleigh, NC, and Boise, ID first?” Use Denver as the primary city and the three listed comparisons. Confirm that this is relevant to the buyer and only use non-sensitive decision details. A participant may stop after the free brief. This question is neither founder-approved nor agreed with a buyer yet.

1. **Approve the exact scope.** Record B01’s eligibility, question, permission for private payment and app reconciliation, total ceiling and observer. Verify the active Hosting release, the selected report’s price and mode, current payment method and tax settings, and that the selected decision has no prior payment or open attempt. The one-time source does not explicitly enable `automatic_tax`. Have the founder confirm applicable tax treatment and the allowed total before the run. Check the actual tax line and total in hosted Stripe Checkout before the buyer pays. The expected product price is **USD $79 once, plus any applicable tax approved in advance**. If the merchant, mode, item, tax or total differs, stop before payment.
2. **Give the buyer the disclosure.** “If you choose to pay, this is a real, one-time $79 USD charge plus any approved tax displayed in Stripe. Please review the total before confirming. You can stop at the free brief or cancel before paying. Enter payment details only on Stripe. We’ll check whether the report arrives and can be reopened.” Confirm affirmative consent to the displayed total and observation. The buyer completes their own payment. Use one Checkout tab and one submission for the entire run. Keep the original tab if the return stalls.
3. **Create and inspect the free brief.** Start at the public sample, use the buyer’s own verified account, and enter the approved question and cities. Capture the resulting decision reference privately. Ask the buyer to state what the free brief establishes, what the paid report adds, and what question they expect the report to help answer. Stop if the free brief or the offer does not support that expectation. Select **Buy Decision Report** only after authorization. Confirm the hosted domain, one-time line item, and approved total. The buyer enters payment details privately.
4. **Reconcile the transaction.** In the correct live Stripe account, check one PaymentIntent succeeded with an actually paid Checkout session, USD amount, tax and matching report reference. Check the successful delivery for the matching signed `checkout.session.completed` or `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded` event to the production commerce endpoint. Verify application processing and exactly one fulfilled payment ledger record and entitlement for the buyer’s decision. Record elapsed time to confirmation. A Checkout redirect is only navigation. [Stripe fulfillment guidance](https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/fulfillment.md?ui=full-page).
5. **Verify delivery and fresh access.** Open the existing decision when confirmation says it is ready. If shown **Prepare full Decision Report**, follow it once and record whether generation completes. Confirm the original question and cities, all selected comparisons, source dates and attribution, uncertainty, and actionable next steps. Ask the buyer what they would do next in their own words. Sign out and use a fresh browser profile or private window to sign in again. Reopen the exact decision via **Your decisions**. Verify full access without a second payment or a second generation prompt.
6. **Stop and reconcile on failure.** Stop on an unexpected price or mode, duplicate or unexplained payment, missing or mismatched webhook, inaccessible report, wrong decision, misleading evidence, or withdrawal of consent. For a pending return, check Stripe and the app after a proposed 10-minute observation window or an agreed shorter support threshold. Keep a pending or indeterminate payment open for reconciliation. Never repeat Checkout. A confirmed payment with failed fulfillment requires operator review of the matching event, report status and existing logs before any corrected delivery or refund. Never grant access or replay a webhook manually without separate authorization.
7. **Refund only with separate founder authorization.** If the paid attempt needs a refund, confirm the exact charge, refund amount and reason privately, check for an existing refund or dispute, and use the original payment in Stripe. Confirm the provider refund state and separately reconcile app access, ledger and reporting under the agreed policy. The legacy one-time path has no automatic refund-to-entitlement reversal in `monetization.ts`. Do not assume a refund removes access or that gross collections equal net revenue. Exclude refunds, duplicates, internal and test activity from the independent count. [Stripe refunds](https://docs.stripe.com/refunds).

Record one row of aggregate evidence: attempt code, mode, consent, price correct, payment confirmed, webhook processed, unlock correct, report ready, buyer’s takeaway coded, reopen passed, time to payment/fulfillment, disposition and remaining incident. Keep all raw IDs, timestamps tied to identities, and buyer notes in restricted records. Never collect card details, personal questions or real bill data in the handoff.

## Analytics audit

| Requested step | Coverage in the deployed code | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sample viewed | No sample event. The public sample returns before `report_view`. | The authenticated `app_open` event cannot count public sample visitors. |
| Free decision started | No distinct event. | `app_open` means the workspace home loaded. |
| Free decision completed | `decision_created` comes after a successful save, with choice type and comparison count. | Counts successful creates across eligible plans. It does not distinguish a free cohort on its own. |
| Paid report offer viewed | `pricing_view` on the pricing route, with the highlighted offer. | A page-load proxy. The public and inline result offers have no direct impression event. |
| Checkout started | `checkout_started` is emitted before the create call. | An attempt can be denied or fail before Stripe creates a session. |
| Checkout completed | `checkout_success` reflects a return query string. The dashboard labels this **Checkout → return**. | The internal ratio field is named `checkoutToPaid`, but it uses return events from distinct organizations in the time window. It cannot prove a Stripe completion, settlement, or matched conversion. |
| Payment confirmed | Signed webhook writes `stripePayments` by Checkout Session ID with live/test and fulfillment outcome. | Route refresh does not confirm payment. Replays of one session update the same ledger entry. Two paid sessions for the same decision can appear as two payments and one duplicate. Include provider/refund reconciliation before counting customers or net revenue. |
| Report generated | State is persisted with the report, with no dedicated usage event. | A precomputed report and an optional post-payment generation have different timelines. Read persisted state for B01. |
| Report opened | `report_view` on private result load, with a coarse paid tag. | It covers free, paid and repeatedly viewed results. A paid entitlement flag can precede deferred generation. |
| Report reopened | No distinct event. | Repeat loads cannot establish a fresh session or reopening of the same report from these events alone. |

The dashboard’s raw counts can increase on reload or retry. Its organization-level de-duplication reduces repeated events but does not join a decision, Checkout session, and entitlement. The payment ledger distinguishes provider confirmation from app return. A recorded payment can still have a missing or duplicate fulfillment outcome. Existing call sites send short categorical values such as offer, type and paid status. The backend limits length and accepts generic short string context, rather than an event-specific key/value allowlist. Preserve the current categorical call sites. Introduce strict per-event allowlists and local tests before any future telemetry expansion, and keep identifiers needed for operations in restricted ledgers. Current analytics cannot establish a full paid conversion rate.

## Report quality from synthetic fixtures

The same synthetic four-city decision returns one visible comparison and no full report before unlocking. The browser fixture verifies full report rendering, copy and print on desktop and mobile. The shared report UI displays the question and supplied cities, source labels and citations, limitations, an action plan, and what could change the decision. The current report does not measure competition, local commercial rent, foot traffic, or concept-level demand. The limitation copy also explains that ACS margins of error are not displayed. The action plan should direct a reader to confirm these local factors. The extra comparisons and action plan constitute more product content. Their value at $79 remains a buyer observation. Synthetic fixtures provide no verified claims about any named market.
